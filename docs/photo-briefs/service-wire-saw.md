## The picture this page needs

The argument on this page is that a disc runs out of depth and a wire does not: the wire is threaded
through pre-drilled holes, closed into a loop around the element, and pulled through a beam, a column,
a foundation or a thick retaining wall. Nobody outside the trade has seen this. **The unfamiliar
geometry is the whole value of the photograph** — a steel wire wrapped around a metre-thick beam is an
image a contractor remembers.

## What is in frame

The **element being cut — beam, column, foundation, retaining wall — with the wire looped around it**,
running over its guide pulleys, entering and leaving through the drilled feed holes. The **drive unit
standing back at a distance**, its wire run visible between it and the element. Water on the cut line.
The **barrier tape or fencing along the wire path** in the shot, doing its job.

The best moment is either **the rig set up and threaded before the run starts**, or a frame taken
**from outside the barrier while it runs**. Both are honest. Both are safe.

Show the scale: a wire around a slim column looks like a cable tie; a wire around a beam you cannot
put your arms round looks like the specialist job it is. If there is a way to include something the
eye knows for size — a rail, a doorway, a prop, a standard block — include it.

## Where the camera stands

**Outside the barriered zone, along the beam rather than square to it**, phone landscape, chest
height, five to eight metres back — far enough that the loop, the pulleys and the drive unit are all
in one frame and the relationship between them is legible.

Then one closer frame **from the same safe side**: the wire entering the concrete at the cut, the kerf
it has already opened behind it, water running out of the slot.

Light: whatever the site has; basements and undercrofts will be dark, so light from the side rather
than from the camera, and keep the phone still — a long exposure of a moving wire is a grey smear.

## What must survive the cover crop

- **The wrapped element sits in the middle band** and the wire loop stays complete inside the frame.
  A loop with a side cropped off stops reading as a loop.
- Keep clear margin at the frame edges; the cover crop takes them and nothing here can be recovered.
- At 320px the wire itself is about one pixel wide. It reads because of the **shapes it makes** — the
  arc round the element, the pulleys, the line running back to the drive unit. Compose those shapes
  large and clean, against a background that contrasts with them.
- Landscape only. This is a wide subject.

## What must stay out of frame

- **Any person inside the wire path while it is running.** This is not a framing preference: the page
  states the route is fenced and clear of people during the cut, and a photograph showing otherwise
  contradicts the site's own safety copy and is unpublishable.
- Any face other than אור שוורץ's. Infrastructure sites are full of other people — wait for a clear
  frame.
- Licence plates, site hoarding naming the developer or the project, project boards, engineering
  drawings with a title block, delivery notes.
- Any certificate, licence, insurance paper, badge, rating or review.
- A load-bearing element already cut through with **no temporary support visible** — that reads as
  exactly the thing the page says never happens without an engineer.

## How to send it

Original file, largest size, no crop, no filter. Send with it: **what the element is, its thickness,
the length of the cut**, and confirmation that the client and the main contractor agreed to
publication. If the job was under a signed engineering plan — as it will have been — you can say so in
a message, but nothing about the plan goes in the caption, and no part of the plan goes in the frame.
